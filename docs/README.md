# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory contains OctoAcme's project management processes, from validating a new initiative through delivery, release, and continuous improvement. The guidance applies to cross-functional projects that deliver product features, services, or integrations and emphasizes customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety.

## Project lifecycle and team practices

Work starts with **initiation**, where the team confirms the business need, measurable outcomes, stakeholders, initial risks, and resources before deciding whether to proceed. During **planning**, the team turns an approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, a Definition of Done, and a milestone or release plan. In **execution**, the delivery team works in small increments, tracks work on a project board, reviews progress and blockers regularly, and demonstrates completed work. Before **release**, the team verifies acceptance criteria, CI and security checks, release notes, rollback plans, and smoke tests; after deployment, it verifies the release and communicates it to stakeholders. **Retrospectives** after sprints, releases, milestones, or incidents turn lessons into owned, time-bound improvement actions.

The **Project Manager (PM)** coordinates plans, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications. The **Product Manager (PdM)** defines outcomes and success measures and prioritizes the roadmap and backlog. **Developers** implement and test the work, while **QA/Testing** validates quality and acceptance criteria; stakeholders provide input and approvals. The PM and PdM align weekly, delivery teams hold standups at the agreed cadence, stakeholders receive regular updates, and teams use demos and reviews to share progress and gather feedback.

Teams maintain a risk register and review risks and dependencies regularly, escalating blockers from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor as needed. Quality practices include unit tests for new logic, integration tests where applicable, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows before release, CI testing and linting, security scanning, and manual QA when needed. Teams track delivery and product signals, communicate status through a shared source of truth, and use retrospectives to measure and improve their processes.
